hexagram 0 (King Wen no. 2) — The Receptive

Judgement — Ground. The soil receives, and form settles into place. Nothing has yet happened, and yet everything is already made possible: the earth of the first line demands nothing, it waits. This is not the passivity of one who gives up, but the patience of one who knows that rightly understood docility carries within it all the fruitfulness to come. The whole figure will unfold from this one quality alone: to welcome before wishing to impose, to settle before presuming to build.

Image. Earth carries and nourishes without distinction: it does not choose what it supports, it supports everything. The broken line twice over, the same openness twice, one welcoming the other without ever closing. This is the image of pure capacity — not the one that acts first, but the one that makes possible the action of all that comes to it. The sage who contemplates this figure understands that generosity needs no brilliance: thus the noble one welcomes all beings with generous virtue, as vast as the soil that refuses no seed.

hexagram 63 (King Wen no. 1) — The Creative

Judgement — Onset. The unbroken line surges forth alone, not yet clothed in form. At this very first degree of the figure, the power already exists in full, yet it has taken on no recognizable shape — it wears no function, no role. This is force in its pure state, before it has given itself a name. This line announces what the five that follow will be: a progression in which the power to act gradually fashions itself a garment, until the whole figure, at its summit, discovers that even the greatest force must learn to hold itself back.

Image. Heaven moves without ceasing above Heaven: the unbroken line twice over, the same force twice, one carrying the other without ever faltering. Nothing here limits the movement; nothing stops it. This is the image of pure vigor, the kind that knows no rest because it has not yet met what could resist it. The sage who contemplates this figure does not see an invitation to agitation, but a model: just as Heaven turns without interruption, the noble one never stops strengthening himself — not through occasional effort, but through a discipline as continuous as the movement of the stars.
