hexagram 29 (King Wen no. 49) — Revolution

Judgement — Momentum. The fire beneath the lake dissolves the old skin. Every deep change first requires the old form to dissolve, however uncomfortable that moment of transition may be. This line says the rightest revolution is never gratuitous — it answers a necessity the old form could no longer contain.

Image. Within the Lake, Fire: the image of revolution. Flame burns beneath the water's surface, two natures that cannot coexist without one eventually transforming the other — a confrontation that necessarily leads to a change of state. Thus the noble one sets the calendar in order and makes the seasons clear: true revolution does not destroy for destruction's sake, it reorders what the old time had left confused.

hexagram 34 (King Wen no. 4) — Youthful Folly

Judgement — Apprenticeship. The disciple's ignorance calls the master forth. It is not a weakness to not yet know — it is an opening: the one who does not yet know is precisely the one who can learn. The whole figure turns on this necessary meeting between the inexperience that seeks and the experience that answers — without this first humility, no teaching can take hold.

Image. At the foot of the mountain a spring wells up: the image of youthful folly. The water is there, lively, but it does not yet know where to flow; it has strength without direction. This is the portrait of inexperience, not as a flaw but as a state — the state before learning, when energy overflows without discernment. Thus the noble one, recognizing this figure in himself or in another, does not despise ignorance: he nourishes his character through resolute action, knowing that the spring, well guided, will become a river.
