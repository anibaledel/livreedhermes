hexagram 9 (King Wen no. 51) — The Arousing

Judgement — Jolt. What remains standing after the shock is what was truly possessed. The shock creates nothing, it reveals: what collapses at the first tremor was never as solid as it seemed. This line says trial is always, in some way, a revealer of truth.

Image. Thunder doubled: the image of shock. The blow strikes twice, one echoing the other, and what remains standing after such a jolt finally reveals its true solidity. This is the figure of trial that tests without destroying what is truly well-founded. Thus the noble one, in fear and trembling, examines and cultivates his own conduct: the outer shock, however brutal, can become the occasion for a salutary inner reckoning.

hexagram 54 (King Wen no. 57) — The Gentle, Wind

Judgement — Weaving. The wind penetrates everywhere without a sound, like the weft. The most discreet force is sometimes the most effective: it meets no head-on resistance because it never announces itself as force. This line teaches the power of continuous, silent influence.

Image. Winds that follow one another: the image of the gentle doubled. Wind blows, then blows again, insisting without ever striking — an influence that penetrates through repetition rather than force. Thus the noble one spreads his orders abroad and carries his affairs into effect: what cannot be imposed all at once often ends up settling in through the gentle constancy of patient insistence.
