Tradition

The Yi Jing judgement and image already on this site, quoted as they stand (source: scripts/extract-hexagram-data.js (tirage-livree-hermes.html)). This is not an attribution to the form.

hexagram 31 (King Wen no. 43) — Breakthrough

Judgement — Distillation. The decision that cuts clean and eliminates the superfluous. There comes a moment when hesitation itself becomes more dangerous than the sharpness of the decision. This line says true breakthrough does not consist in destroying everything, but in precisely removing what, in excess, threatened the whole.

Image. The Lake rises above Heaven: the image of breakthrough. Water, carried higher than what should normally contain it, threatens to overflow — an extreme tension that calls for a clear, irreversible decision. This is the figure of resolution that can wait no longer. Thus the noble one spreads his bounty downward, never resting on virtue alone: cutting decisively must always be accompanied by generosity toward those the decision affects.

hexagram 32 (King Wen no. 23) — Splitting Apart

Judgement — Mutation. The collapse of the old edifice frees the energy of new growth. What crumbles does not take everything down with it: the ruin of an exhausted form releases the space and energy needed for what follows. This line teaches not to fear the splitting apart of structures grown hollow — it always precedes renewal.

Image. The mountain rests upon the Earth: the image of splitting apart. What seemed most solid — the mountain's own mass — is in fact only sustained by a base that can give way. This is the figure of collapse awaiting whoever neglects its foundations. Thus the great secure what lies below, that their own house may find rest: before building higher, one must always strengthen what carries the weight.
