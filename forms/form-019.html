hexagram 18 (King Wen no. 29) — The Abysmal

Judgement — Trial. Navigating water that will not be sounded. Certain depths can never be fully measured in advance — one can only learn to move within them with what means are at hand, never claiming to master them entirely. This line says that true skill before danger is not to eliminate it, but to learn to swim in it.

Image. Water comes and comes again: the image of the abysmal doubled. Danger repeats, wave after wave, never offering complete respite — yet water itself, in its constant motion, always finds its own level. This is the figure of repeated trial that forges constancy. Thus the noble one steadily practices virtue and teaches the ways of things: facing danger that returns, only repeated practice, in turn, builds mastery.

hexagram 45 (King Wen no. 30) — That Which Clings, Fire

Judgement — Radiance. The clarity of fire clings to what lives, to spread its glow. Nothing shines in a vacuum: all true brightness needs an anchor, a matter to bind itself to in order to unfold. This line teaches that total independence is not an ideal — even the purest light needs something to illuminate.

Image. Brightness, and brightness again: the image of Fire that clings. Light never shines alone — it needs something to hold onto in order to keep radiating, just as flame needs wood. This is the figure of brilliance dependent on a support. Thus the great man, through his unbroken radiance, illuminates the four horizons: to shine lastingly requires staying attached to what nourishes one's own light.
