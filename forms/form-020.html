hexagram 19 (King Wen no. 60) — Limitation

Judgement — Containment. The bamboo sets its nodes so the sap will not disperse. Without the limits that punctuate it, growth itself would lose its strength by scattering without direction. This line says well-placed limitation is not a brake on life, but the condition of its vigor.

Image. Upon the Lake, Water: the image of limitation. The lake's water stays contained by its banks, and it is this very limit that allows it to exist as a lake rather than losing itself as a shapeless marsh. This is the figure of constraint that structures rather than stifles. Thus the noble one devises measure and number, and reflects on virtue and conduct: rightly chosen limitation is what gives form to everything, including oneself.

hexagram 44 (King Wen no. 56) — The Wanderer

Judgement — Wayfaring. With neither hearth nor home, the livery stands in for the house. When a dwelling is lacking, it is the garment, the bearing, the conduct that must take its place — a dignity no longer dependent on place but on manner of being. This line says the most solid identity is the one carried with oneself, independent of any outer anchor.

Image. On the mountain, Fire: the image of the wanderer. Flame moves along the slope, lingering nowhere, consuming what presents itself without ever taking root in one place. This is the figure of one with neither hearth nor fixed abode. Thus the noble one applies punishment with clarity and caution, letting no case drag on: without lasting attachments, all the more rigor is needed not to lose one's way along the journey.
