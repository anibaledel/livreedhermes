Tradition

hexagram 26 (King Wen no. 47) — Oppression

Judgement — Repair. Nothing is restored without first consenting to lack. There is no true rebirth without first having passed through the real experience of deprivation. This line says that oppression, well endured, is not merely a trial to suffer but a purification that prepares the return of abundance.

Image. The Lake is without water: the image of oppression. What should nourish and sustain has dried up, leaving behind a glaring lack where abundance was expected. This is the figure of exhaustion that tests the true nature of the one who endures it. Thus the noble one stakes his life to accomplish his purpose: it is precisely in lack that what one truly values is revealed, to the point of being ready to risk everything for it.

hexagram 37 (King Wen no. 22) — Grace

Judgement — Distillation. Ornament and beauty of form yield, at the extreme, to decision. All beauty has its useful limit: past a certain point, ornament must give way to the necessity of acting. This line reminds us that form serves substance, never the reverse — the moment one encroaches on the other, simplicity must be restored.

Image. Beneath the mountain, Fire: the image of grace. The flame lights the mountain's flanks without ever claiming to equal its height — an adornment that respects what it reveals rather than dominating it. This is the figure of ornament in service of substance. Thus the noble one sheds light on the many affairs of the day, yet never presumes to settle disputes by appearances alone: grace serves to reveal, never to judge.
