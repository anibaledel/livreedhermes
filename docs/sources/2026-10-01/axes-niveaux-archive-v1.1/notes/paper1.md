# Abstract

We describe a finite construction of straight-line figures on a square, and we
establish that its vocabulary is **generated** rather than chosen.

One operation suffices, and the origin is forced rather than chosen. The
operation is the doubling of the offset about the centre of the square; the
vocabulary is the smallest set of lines stable under the inverse of that
doubling. A level fixed by the doubling has no preimage outside itself, so it
cannot be generated and must be posited, while every other level is reachable:
the origin is therefore the set of fixed levels, and there are exactly two of
them, $0^\circ$ and $120^\circ$, the second existing precisely because $3$
divides $12$. Started from those two, the orthogonal vocabulary closes in two
steps on $24$ offsets, exactly the union of the eight orthogonal families; the
diagonal vocabulary closes in three steps, also on $24$, the union of the eight
diagonal families together with one further offset — the inscribed diamond,
which belongs to the vocabulary and is not drawn.

The first generation of that closure — four families — is not an arbitrary
stopping point: its fifteen non-empty unions are, one for one, the fifteen
families of a pattern system fixed by hand years earlier, and the check against
the sixty published images succeeds sixty times out of sixty.

Three consequences follow, none of them stipulated. The backward structure is
not one chain but two towers of height two, rooted at the two fixed levels, and
at one place only — the top of the first tower — the two branches of the inverse
doubling coincide with the division of a level into a *base* and a *mutant*; what
holds at every level is the shift $e \mapsto e+3 \pmod 6$, verified on all four, and it is that shift
which defines the mutation. The doubling has exactly four fixed families, and an
explicit criterion — verified on all $65\,535$ subsets without exception —
decides when iterating it leaves the vocabulary.
Finally, the parity rule that turns a set of axes into a figure is reversible: it
is injective on band systems outright, and on the plates tested a figure
determines the chord that produced it up to the three relations the families
satisfy.

We also show that the cell on which the parity is read is a parameter and not a
constant: the same axes and the same rule, read on a triangle subdivision or on
the square cell, produce two figures that share no information, and we give the
scalar that says which reading carries content. The integrity check the
construction affords is a linear code: the sixteen family figures span
$[1152, 13]$ with minimum distance $288$ on the triangle reading and $[144, 12]$
with distance $36$ on the cell reading, so the corpus corrects errors and does not
merely detect them. And the period $12$ is itself constrained: the descent
terminates without a cycle exactly for $N = 2^a$ or $3\cdot 2^a$, and a
non-trivial fixed level exists exactly when $3 \mid N$; requiring in addition a
depth of at least two leaves $12, 24, 48$.

Version 1.1 adds two things. The cell reading $C_1$ now states its rule for
axes that pass through cell centres — such an axis does not separate the cell,
which closes the figure continuously — and every $C_1$ number depends on it
(§5.4). And a **second cut** of the diagonal vocabulary is examined (§5.5):
twelve elements, YA1–YA6 and AY1–AY6, which partition the $288$ cell diagonals
of the square and which, read as band systems, give exactly **one generator per
node of the doubling tree**. The first cut resolves one tower node by node and
the other only by pairs; the second resolves both. The code passes from
$[1152,13,288]$ to $[1152,15,288]$ on $C_8$ and from $[144,12,36]$ to
$[144,14,36]$ on $C_1$; it contains the old code, keeps the same minimum-weight
words, and has no relation among its diagonal generators. On the second cut the
mutation is the shift $e\mapsto e+3$ on the diagonals as well as on the
orthogonal levels.

This is a working basis and not a finished result. We claim no depth for the
construction and make no physical claim; the comparison with the nodal and
iso-amplitude figures of a vibrating plate is the subject of a separate paper,
which uses the vocabulary established here and nothing else from it.

# 1. The square, the natures, the offset

Let $P$ be the square $[0,12]^2$ divided into $144$ unit cells.

An **axis** is a straight line drawn on $P$ in one of four **natures** —
horizontal, vertical, and the two diagonal directions. It is specified by its
signed **offset** $e$ from the centre, measured in cells; for the diagonal
natures the offset is the perpendicular coordinate rescaled to cells, that is
$(x+y-12)/2$ and $(y-x)/2$. Offsets lie in $(-6,6]$, with $6$ and $-6$ denoting
the same line, and two axes of the same nature and different offsets are
different lines.

Two subdivisions of $P$ are used. **$C_1$** takes each cell as one region, giving
$144$ regions. The test point of a region and the rule for an axis passing through it are fixed
in §5.4. **$C_8$** cuts each cell by its two diagonals and its two medians
into eight triangles, giving $1152$ regions; a region is indexed by $(r,c,s)$
with $s$ the $45^\circ$ sector clockwise from the upward vertical. The sectors
are not angularly uniform — the centroids fall at $\pm 26.57^\circ$ and
$\pm 63.43^\circ$ — so a sector must be assigned by
$s = \lfloor \theta/45 \rfloor$ and not by rounding to the nearest multiple.

A **figure** is a two-colouring of the regions of one subdivision.

![**Figure 1.** The sixteen families, drawn on the square. The frame is the
boundary of $P$; an axis lying on it is drawn in full. Counted as segments there
are $94$ distinct lines here, thirty of them appearing in two
families.](figures/fig2-familles.svg)

# 2. The vocabulary is generated

Let $\sigma$ be the doubling of the offset about the centre,

$$ \sigma : e \longmapsto 2e \pmod{12}, $$

acting within each nature. It is the homothety of ratio $2$ read on offsets. It
is two-to-one: every offset has exactly two preimages, $e/2$ and $e/2 + 6$.

**Definition.** *Given a set $\Omega$ of offsets in one nature, the* vocabulary
generated by $\Omega$ *is the smallest set containing $\Omega$ and stable under
$\sigma^{-1}$.*

Nothing else is stipulated: there is no free parameter in this definition, hence
nothing that could have been tuned.

What is to be taken as origin is not free either, and this is the point of the
section. A level fixed by $\sigma$ has no preimage outside itself, so it cannot
be reached by any number of backward steps: it must be posited. Conversely,
every level that is *not* fixed is reachable. The origin is therefore forced to
be the set of fixed levels, and Theorem 8 of §6 says what that set is: the level
$0^\circ$ always, and the level $120^\circ$ exactly because $3$ divides $12$.

In each nature those two levels are two of the sixteen families:

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{orthogonal} & 0^\circ = T_0\,\mathrm{YIN}\ \{0,6\} &
 120^\circ = T_2\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}\ \{\pm2,\pm4\} \\
\text{diagonal} & 0^\circ = T_0\,\mathrm{YANG}\ \{0\} &
 120^\circ = T_2\,\mathrm{YANG}\ \{\pm1,\pm2,\pm4\}
\end{array}
$$

**Theorem 1 (orthogonal).** *The vocabulary generated by the two fixed
orthogonal levels, $\Omega_{o} = \{0,6\} \cup \{\pm2,\pm4\}$, reaches a fixed
point in two steps, on $24$ offsets. That fixed point is exactly the union of
the eight orthogonal families of the system.*

The sizes are $6,\ 12,\ 24$.

**Theorem 2 (diagonal).** *The vocabulary generated by the two fixed diagonal
levels, $\Omega_{d} = \{0\} \cup \{\pm1,\pm2,\pm4\}$, reaches a fixed point in
three steps, also on $24$ offsets. That fixed point is the union of the eight
diagonal families together with the single offset $6$.*

The sizes are $7,\ 14,\ 20,\ 24$.

Neither level generates the other, and neither suffices alone. The $0^\circ$
level closes, in *either* nature, on the same eight offsets
$\{0,\ \pm1.5,\ \pm3,\ \pm4.5,\ 6\}$ — four families orthogonally, three plus
the diamond diagonally — and stops there, because $\pm1.5$ has no preimage on
the half-integer grid. The $120^\circ$ level closes on sixteen offsets
orthogonally and never produces $\pm1.5$ at all. The two towers are disjoint
above their roots and their union is the whole vocabulary. This is why §2.1
below concerns the $0^\circ$ tower alone: the fifteen families of the existing
system are generated by that root, and the $120^\circ$ tower is what the plates
of the treatise add to it.

**The offset $6$.** In the orthogonal natures it is the edge of $P$, drawn; in
the diagonal natures it is the inscribed diamond, in the vocabulary but *not*
drawn. In both it is the second preimage of $0$, the one that the fixed level
does not already contain, and it is what lets the $0^\circ$ tower rise at all.
Forbidding it at every step costs exactly the same seven offsets in both
natures — $\{\pm1.5,\ \pm3,\ \pm4.5,\ 6\}$, the whole tower above its root —
and leaves $17$ offsets instead of $24$. Orthogonally that is $T_0$ YIN MUT,
$T_1$ YIN and $T_1$ YIN MUT; diagonally, $T_0$ YANG MUT and $T_1$ YANG MUT. The
two natures have the same vocabulary of $24$ offsets, the same origin
structure and the same operation, and differ only in the status of that one
offset — drawn on one side, an invisible generator on the other.

## 2.1 The first generation is the vocabulary of an existing system

The first step of the $0^\circ$ tower orthogonally, and its second step
diagonally, produce four families: $T_0$ YIN, $T_0$ YIN MUT, $T_0$ YANG and
$T_0$ YANG MUT.
They are pairwise disjoint, so under symmetric difference they generate a space
isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$, whose fifteen non-zero elements are the fifteen
non-empty unions.

Those fifteen unions are not an abstraction. The pattern system from which this
vocabulary is drawn was designed by hand and organised, long before any of this,
into fifteen families of four *natures* — sixty images in all. Checked against
the published data:

- every one of the sixty images has one of its three tints occupying exactly the
  forty-eight cells whose centre lies on an axis of $T_1$ YANG, the same set for
  all sixty (thirty images in one tint, thirty in another);
- the remaining ninety-six cells of every image are, in every case, the parity
  figure of one of the fifteen unions of $T_0$ families, split between the two
  remaining tints.

Sixty out of sixty, by exhaustive comparison. **The fifteen families of the
existing system are the fifteen non-empty subsets of the four families of the
first generation**, and the four natures are the four tint permutations that
distinguish the readings.

This is the one justification we can give for the origin of §2. It is not that
$\{0,6\}$ is canonical among all possible origins; it is that its first
generation reproduces, exactly and without residue, a vocabulary that was fixed
independently and beforehand.

Counted as drawn lines across the four natures and the sixteen families, the
vocabulary has **94 distinct axes**. Thirty of them belong to two families each,
and all thirty are diagonal; no orthogonal axis is shared.

# 3. Two towers, and what separates base from mutant

The backward structure is not a single chain. Written out in full, the
orthogonal preimages are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^{-1}(T_0\,\mathrm{YIN}) &= T_0\,\mathrm{YIN} \cup T_0\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \\
\sigma^{-1}(T_0\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}) &= T_1\,\mathrm{YIN} \cup T_1\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \\
\sigma^{-1}(T_1\,\mathrm{YIN}) &= \sigma^{-1}(T_1\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}) = \varnothing \\
\sigma^{-1}(T_2\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}) &= T_2\,\mathrm{YIN} \cup T_2\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \\
\sigma^{-1}(T_2\,\mathrm{YIN}) &= T_3\,\mathrm{YIN} \cup T_3\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \\
\sigma^{-1}(T_3\,\mathrm{YIN}) &= \sigma^{-1}(T_3\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}) = \varnothing
\end{aligned}
$$

so there is no chain $T_0 \to T_1 \to T_2 \to T_3$. There are two towers of
height two, rooted at the two fixed levels of §2:

$$
T_0\,\mathrm{YIN} \to T_0\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \to
\{T_1\,\mathrm{YIN},\ T_1\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}\},
\qquad
T_2\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \to T_2\,\mathrm{YIN} \to
\{T_3\,\mathrm{YIN},\ T_3\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}\}.$$

The names $T_0,\dots,T_3$ index levels of $\varphi$, not generations of
$\sigma$, and the two orderings do not agree; we keep the names because the
existing system uses them.

Since $\sigma$ is two-to-one, each preimage set splits into two branches, and at
exactly one place in the structure that split coincides with the division into a
**base** and a **mutant**: the top of the first tower. The two preimages of $3$
are $1.5$, in $T_1$ YIN, and $-4.5$, in $T_1$ YIN MUT. Nowhere else. At the root
of that tower the two preimages of $0$ are $0$ and $6$, both in $T_0$ YIN; at the
root of the second, the preimages of $2$ are $1$ and $-5$, both in $T_2$ YIN; and
at the *top* of the second the branches do not separate the families either — the
preimages of $1$ are $0.5$ and $-5.5$, both in $T_3$ YIN, and those of $5$ are
$2.5$ and $-3.5$, both in $T_3$ YIN MUT. So the inverse doubling produces the
base/mutant division once, by accident of where the offsets fall, and it is the
following that holds throughout.

![**Figure 2.** The two orthogonal towers. Boxes are families, arrows are
$\sigma^{-1}$, and a double border marks a level fixed by $\sigma$. The descent
of §4 runs against the arrows.](figures/fig1-tours.svg)

**Proposition 1.** *On the orthogonal levels the mutant is obtained from the
base by $e \mapsto e+3 \pmod 6$: for each level $t$, the offsets of the mutant
are those of the base shifted by $3$ modulo $6$. Verified on all four levels.*

This, and not the inverse doubling, is what defines the mutation. The relation
fails on the diagonal families of the first cut, and §2 says why: each of them
already carries both branches, so no shift can separate what is not separated.
It is a property of the cut and not of the diagonals: on the second cut of §5.5,
which carries one level per generator, the same shift relates every yang element
to its mutable twin.

# 4. The descent, and where it leaves the vocabulary

Iterating $\sigma$ forward instead of backward gives a descent.

**Theorem 3.** *$\sigma$ maps each of the sixteen families into another family of
the vocabulary, with two exceptions. Writing $A \to B$ for
$\sigma(A) \subseteq B$ with $B$ the smallest family containing $\sigma(A)$, and
marking with $=$ the arrows that are equalities,*

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_1\,\mathrm{YIN} \overset{=}{\to} T_0\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT},\quad
T_1\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} &\overset{=}{\to} T_0\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \to T_0\,\mathrm{YIN} \\
T_3\,\mathrm{YIN},\ T_3\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} &\to T_2\,\mathrm{YIN} \to T_2\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT} \\
T_1\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} &\overset{=}{\to} T_0\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} \\
T_2\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT},\ T_3\,\mathrm{YANG} &\to T_3\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} \to T_2\,\mathrm{YANG}
\end{aligned}
$$

*Four arrows are equalities: the three marked above and $T_0$ YANG $\to T_0$
YANG, which is a fixed family. All others are strict inclusions.*

*and the fixed families are exactly*

$$T_0\,\mathrm{YIN}\ \{0,6\},\quad T_0\,\mathrm{YANG}\ \{0\},\quad
T_2\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}\ \{\pm 2,\pm 4\},\quad T_2\,\mathrm{YANG}\ \{\pm1,\pm2,\pm4\}.$$

**Theorem 4.** *The image of a chord under $\sigma$ leaves the vocabulary if and
only if the chord contains $T_0$ YANG MUT or $T_1$ YANG. Verified on all
$65\,535$ chords, with no counterexample.*

The escape is always to the same object, the diagonal offset $6$ — the inscribed
diamond of §2. The generator of the diagonal vocabulary is also the one place
where the descent leaves it.

Two notions of fixity must be kept apart, or the count changes between
statements. Theorem 3 calls a family fixed when the arrow returns to it, that is
when the *smallest* family containing $\sigma(A)$ is $A$ itself; there are four.
The weaker condition $\sigma(A) \subseteq A$ holds for five, the fifth being
$T_3$ YANG MUT, whose image $\{\pm2,\pm4\}$ sits inside its own sixteen axes but
sits inside the twelve of $T_2$ YANG as well, so its arrow points at $T_2$ YANG.
The corollary below uses the weaker condition.

**Corollary.** *Call a chord* harmonically closed *if $\sigma(A) \subseteq A$.
There are $719$ such chords, and they are exactly the chords closed downward
under the descent; the minimal ones are the five families satisfying that
condition —* $T_0$ YIN, $T_0$ YANG, $T_2$ YIN MUT, $T_2$ YANG, $T_3$ YANG MUT. *Harmonic closure is
therefore an operation and not a search: adjoin the images of the members until
the fixed families are reached.*

# 5. The parity, and the cell on which it is read

## 5.1 Drawn lines and stripe systems

A figure is obtained from a chord by a parity rule, and the rule distinguishes
two objects that must not be conflated.

The **catalogue** lists drawn lines: offsets in $(-6,6]$, no folding.

The **parity** operates on periodic stripe systems. Each drawn line is converted
to a key $(\text{nature},\ u \bmod 12)$, where $u$ is the *raw* perpendicular
coordinate — $6+e$ for a horizontal or vertical axis, $12+2e$ for $x+y$, and $2e$
for $y-x$ — and the exclusive-or is taken over the **set** of keys. Because of
the factor $2$, two diagonal offsets differing by $6$ share a key; orthogonal
offsets never collide. Seven of the sixteen families therefore have fewer stripe
systems than drawn lines: $T_2$ YANG has twelve lines and eight systems, and
those eight are exactly the systems of $T_3$ YANG MUT, which is why the two
families have the same figure and why one of the three relations of §5.4 exists
at all.

Deduplication is not cancellation. The parity is an exclusive-or over distinct
keys, never over the list of offsets, and a family read alone gives the same
figure as the same family read inside a union.

## 5.2 A convention-free criterion, and reversibility

The definition above fixes an origin and a global polarity, neither intrinsic.
The following avoids both.

**Definition.** A figure $F$ *is the parity of* a set of axes $A$ if, for every
pair of adjacent regions, the two differ in colour **if and only if** an axis of
$A$ lies along their common edge.

This is differential: insensitive to the origin and invariant under global colour
exchange. On $C_8$ there are $1728$ adjacent pairs, $576$ of them between cells.

**Theorem 5.** *On the plates tested, each figure is the parity of a chord of at
most six families, recovered by exhaustive sweep over the $14\,892$ such chords
at zero discrepancy over $1152$ regions, and that chord is unique modulo the
three relations of Theorem 6 below.*

The qualification is not a formality. Under the canonical rule $T_2$ YANG and
$T_3$ YANG MUT have the same key set and therefore the same figure, as do
$T_3$ YANG and $T_2$ YANG MUT, so a chord containing one member of such a pair
and the chord obtained by swapping it for the other are indistinguishable by
their figure, and no amount of sweeping will separate them. What is true without
qualification is stated in Theorem 7: the parity map is injective on band
systems. Theorem 5 is the restriction of that injectivity to unions of families,
where the fibre is the coset of the relation space.

Uniqueness makes the construction a code rather than a drawing method: a figure
carries its own generating data, recoverable without external reference.

The generative procedure follows: take a chord of two families and compute the
parity; add a third and **recompute** on the union of axes; add a fourth, and so
on. It is not a superposition of the previous figures.

## 5.3 The reading cell is a parameter

Some figures admit no chord on $C_8$ and are exact on $C_1$:

| subdivision | pairs tested | figure A | figure B |
|---|---|---|---|
| $C_8$ | $1728$ | 288 violations | 480 violations |
| $C_1$, between cells | $576$ | **0** | **0** |

Every violation lies on an edge interior to a cell, none between cells, and they
are spread over $120$ and $144$ of the $144$ cells — not a localised defect.

One might expect $C_1$ to be a coarsening of $C_8$. It is not.

**Proposition 2.** *For those chords, each of the $144$ cells contains exactly
four dark and four light triangles in the $C_8$ reading, and the two figures
differ on $576$ of $1152$ regions — exactly half.*

A cell balanced $4/4$ carries no cell-level information, so no coarsening can
recover the $C_1$ figure; and half is the maximum possible discrepancy. The two
readings are independent channels of the same data: $C_8$ carries information
only inside cells, $C_1$ only between them.

The number of $4/4$-balanced cells measures which reading carries content:

| chord | balanced cells |
|---|---|
| $T_0$ YIN, $T_0$ YIN MUT, $T_2$ YIN, $T_2$ YIN MUT | $0/144$ |
| $T_0$ YANG, $T_0$ YANG MUT | $24/144$ |
| $T_1$ YIN, $T_1$ YIN MUT | $44/144$ |
| $T_1$ YANG, $T_1$ YANG MUT | $48/144$ |
| $T_3$ YIN, $T_3$ YIN MUT | $80/144$ |
| $T_2$ YANG, $T_2$ YANG MUT, $T_3$ YANG, $T_3$ YANG MUT | $96/144$ |

No single family reaches $144$. The choice of reading cell is therefore not a
matter of taste: at $0/144$ the triangle reading carries everything, at $144/144$
it carries nothing at cell level, and the scalar interpolates.

## 5.4 The integrity check is a linear code

The consistency property is not merely that each figure is the exclusive-or of
two others. The figures of the sixteen families span a linear code over
$\mathbb{F}_2$, and its parameters are computable.

**The rule for axes through cell centres.** On $C_8$ the test point of a region
is its centroid, chosen so that it lies on no axis. On $C_1$ the test point is
the cell centre $(c+\tfrac12,\,r+\tfrac12)$, where $x+y$ and $y-x$ are
integers, so that every diagonal key and every orthogonal key at a half-integer
offset — $48$ of the $72$ — passes through cell centres. Such an axis is treated
apart: it does not separate the cell, which closes the figure *continuously*, on
the side of increasing coordinate, the same all along the axis. In
`regle_parite.py` this is the floor in $\lfloor (u-c)/12 \rfloor \bmod 2$; the
reference-based count of `codes_cles.py` places such cells on the same side, and
the two implementations agree cell by cell on all sixteen families. The rule is
part of the definition of $C_1$ and every $C_1$ number below depends on it: a
rule that sent such a cell to the side of the reference region instead would lose
the all-dark word.

**Theorem 6.** *On $C_8$ the sixteen family figures span a code $[1152, 13]$ whose
minimum distance is $288$, attained by a single word, and these parameters do not
depend on the parity convention. On $C_1$ the minimum distance is $36$ and the
dimension is $12$ if figures carry the polarity the rule gives them, $11$ if a
reference region is required to be light.*

A minimum distance of $288$ detects up to $287$ wrong regions and corrects up to
$143$; at exactly $288$ there is a hole, since a corruption of that size can land
on another codeword. The unique minimum-weight word is
$T_0\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} \oplus T_1\,\mathrm{YIN}$ — the diamond against the
inner square of offsets $\pm1.5$. Naming it requires naming the reference region
from which the parity is counted, here the corner region at the origin: with the
central region as reference every generator that is dark there is complemented,
the code changes, and the minimum-weight word becomes
$T_0\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} \oplus T_1\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}$. The distance $288$ and
the uniqueness do not depend on that choice; the name does. The kernel has dimension $3$ at $C_8$, generated by

$$ T_0\,\mathrm{YANG} + T_0\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} + T_1\,\mathrm{YANG} = 0, \quad
T_2\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} + T_3\,\mathrm{YANG} = 0, \quad
T_2\,\mathrm{YANG} + T_3\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} = 0, $$

the first being the parity translation of the set identity
$T_1\,\mathrm{YANG} = T_0\,\mathrm{YANG} \cup T_0\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT}$. At $C_1$
two further facts hold, and they are distinct. First, a fourth relation appears:
the eight YIN families sum to zero. Their $48$ keys are pairwise disjoint, so
this is a relation in the union semantics as much as in the symmetric
difference, and not an artefact of one of the two. Second, and separately, the
all-dark figure lies in the span on $C_1$ and not on $C_8$; its shortest
expression is
$T_1\,\mathrm{YANG} \oplus T_1\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} \oplus T_2\,\mathrm{YANG}
\oplus T_2\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT}$. So a global inversion is already a combination
of the families at cell level, which it is not at triangle level. Read through
§5.5, those four families hold the seven diagonal levels between them, each
once: the all-dark figure on $C_1$ is the sum of all the diagonal levels.

The dimension $13$ is not only computed; it is read off the levels. Across the
diagonals the level of an axis of offset $e$ is $\cos(\pi e/3)$, well defined
modulo the period $6$, and the eight diagonal families occupy five distinct sets
of levels — $0^\circ$ ($T_0$ YANG), $180^\circ$ ($T_0$ YANG MUT), $90^\circ$
($T_1$ YANG MUT), $\{60^\circ,120^\circ\}$ ($T_2$ YANG and $T_3$ YANG MUT) and
$\{30^\circ,150^\circ\}$ ($T_2$ YANG MUT and $T_3$ YANG), with $T_1$ YANG the
union of the first two. So the diagonal families have rank $5$, the eight
orthogonal ones rank $8$ on $C_8$, and $5+8=13$; the three relations are the
three diagonal families that repeat what is already spanned. On $C_1$ the
orthogonal rank drops to $7$ — the fourth relation — and $5+7=12$.

That second fact is what makes the dimension at $C_1$ depend on the convention,
and it is worth stating rather than hiding behind a choice. Requiring a reference
region to be light quotients the code by the all-dark vector; on $C_8$ that vector
is not in the span and nothing changes, while on $C_1$ it is, and exactly one
dimension is lost — the relation
$T_1\,\mathrm{YANG} \oplus T_1\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT} \oplus T_2\,\mathrm{YANG}
\oplus T_2\,\mathrm{YANG\ MUT}$, which equals the all-dark figure without the
anchoring and zero with it. The eight-YIN relation survives both, since all eight
are light at the reference region. The minimum distance is $36$ either way.

**Theorem 7.** *The $94$ drawn axes give $72$ distinct stripe systems, of which
exactly two have an empty mask on $C_8$ — the two border systems at $u \equiv 0$.
The remaining $70$ are linearly independent.*

So a figure determines its set of stripe systems uniquely, up to the two
invisible border systems, and this holds for an arbitrary set of axes and not
only for a chord. A count sometimes quoted alongside these is $18\,431$; it is
the number of distinct *unions of drawn segments* over the $65\,535$ chords, not
of figures. The figures are $8\,191 = 2^{13}-1$ under the union semantics, which
never produces the empty figure on $C_8$, and $8\,192$ words under symmetric
difference, seven chords — of sizes $2, 2, 3, 4, 5, 5, 7$ — giving the empty
one. Theorem 5 is the restriction of that injectivity to unions of
families.

## 5.5 A second cut: one generator per node of the doubling tree

The diagonal families above are the *first cut* of the diagonal vocabulary. A
second cut, drawn afterwards, redraws it as twelve elements: YA1–YA6 (yang) and
AY1–AY6 (yang mutable), each a set of unit segments along cell diagonals, with
integer vertices (`axes-v2.json`). Write $c=2e$ for the signed offset of a
diagonal line, $x+y=12+c$ or $y-x=c$, so that its level is $\cos(\pi c/6)$.

**Proposition 3.** *(i) The twelve elements are pairwise disjoint, and their
union is exactly the set of the $288$ cell diagonals of the square; YA$k$ and
AY$k$ have $8k$ segments for $k\le5$ and $24$ for $k=6$, and the $4095$ non-empty
unions of elements are $4095$ distinct drawings. (ii) Every segment of YA$k$ lies
on the level $\theta=180^\circ-30k^\circ$ and every segment of AY$k$ on
$\theta=30k^\circ$. (iii) The elements of one level tile its lines exactly: the
level $0^\circ$ is YA6, the level $180^\circ$ is AY6, and for
$\theta = 30j^\circ$, $1\le j\le5$, the level is YA$(6-j)$ $\cup$ AY$j$.
(iv) As band systems YA$(6-j)$ and AY$j$ coincide, so the twelve elements give
seven key sets, one per level. (v) The lines of AY$k$ are those of YA$k$ shifted
by $3$ in offset: on the second cut the mutation is $e\mapsto e+3$, at level
$180^\circ-\theta$, as on the orthogonal levels (Proposition 1).*

*Proof.* Exact check on the segments of `axes-v2.json` (`niveaux_v2.py`).
$\square$

So the second cut is not one more partition of the diagonals: it is one
generator for each node of the doubling tree $\theta\mapsto2\theta$ of §3, read
on the diagonal levels (Figure 3). The tree has two towers. Tower A,
$90^\circ\to180^\circ\to0^\circ$, is resolved node by node already by the first
cut — $T_1$ YANG MUT, $T_0$ YANG MUT, $T_0$ YANG. Tower B,
$\{30^\circ,150^\circ\}\to60^\circ\to120^\circ$, is resolved by the first cut
only by pairs: $T_2$ YANG ($\equiv T_3$ YANG MUT) holds $60^\circ$ and
$120^\circ$ together, $T_2$ YANG MUT ($\equiv T_3$ YANG) holds $30^\circ$ and
$150^\circ$ together. The second cut separates the two fused pairs, and that is
the whole difference.

**Theorem 9.** *Take as generators the seven diagonal levels of the second cut,
$L_0, L_{30},\dots,L_{180}$, and the eight orthogonal families, unchanged, with
the canonical keys and the rule of §5.4.*

- *On $C_8$ they span a code $[1152, 15, 288]$: rank $7+8$, no relation,
  $32\,767$ non-zero words, and a unique minimum-weight word
  $L_{180}\oplus T_1\,\mathrm{YIN}$.*
- *On $C_1$ they span $[144, 14, 36]$: rank $7+7$, one relation — the eight YIN
  families sum to zero — $16\,383$ non-zero words, two minimum-weight words,
  $L_{180}\oplus T_1\,\mathrm{YIN}$ and
  $L_0\oplus L_{30}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{150}\oplus T_1\,\mathrm{YIN\ MUT}$, and
  the all-dark figure is the sum of the seven levels.*
- *Each code contains the first-cut code of Theorem 6.*

*Proof.* Gaussian elimination and full enumeration of the $2^{15}$ words
(`niveaux_v2.py`). $\square$

Three readings. First, $L_{180}$ is the level of offsets $\pm3$, the band systems
of $T_0$ YANG MUT: the minimum-weight word is literally the word of Theorem 6, the
diamond against the inner square, on the same $288$ triangles. Since the old code
is contained in the new and the new adds no lighter word, the distance does not
change, and neither does the correction capacity — $287$ wrong regions detected,
$143$ corrected. What grows is the number of figures, not the capacity. Second,
the two added dimensions are the two fused nodes of tower B, on $C_8$ and on
$C_1$ alike: $13\to15$ and $12\to14$. Third, the three relations of Theorem 6
were the price of the fusion; with one generator per node there is nothing to
relate on the diagonals, and the only relation left is the orthogonal one at
cell level.

A remark on drawings. A level of the second cut is drawn by two elements, which
share its segments between them without overlap, but read as band systems the
two are one key set. So the parity rule does not see
which of the two is drawn: of the $4095$ drawings of the second cut, exactly
$127 = 2^7-1$ are unions of complete level sets, the two elements of a level
taken together or not at all. The distinction between the two elements of a
level lies below the resolution of the parity; the second cut is a drawing tool
as much as a generator set, and the code is taken on the canonical keys.

# 6. Is the period $12$ a choice?

The construction uses the period $12$ throughout. Asking whether another period
would do is a question about the doubling map alone, and it has an answer.

Read the offsets in $\mathbb{Z}/N$ with the identification $e \sim -e$, since a
level is symmetric about the centre.

**Theorem 8.** *The descent under doubling terminates without a non-trivial cycle
if and only if $N = 2^a$ or $N = 3\cdot 2^a$. A fixed level other than the centre
exists if and only if $3$ divides $N$, and it is then the level of offset $N/3$.*

Verified by enumeration for all $N \le 48$. Without the identification
$e \sim -e$ the first statement is false — $N = 12$ then has the cycle
$4 \to -4 \to 4$ — so the identification is a hypothesis of the theorem and not a
convenience.

Consequently a period admits both a terminating descent and a non-trivial fixed
level exactly when $N \in \{3\cdot 2^a\}$, and since the construction needs $N$
even — a half-integer grid, and a diagonal period $N/2$ — that is
$\{6, 12, 24, 48, \dots\}$. Write
$N = 2^a M$ with $M$ odd; the **depth** of the descent, the number of doublings
after which every level is fixed, is $a$. Depth and number of levels are
different quantities and should not be confused: $N = 12$ has seven levels of
$\varphi$, four of which carry the names $T_0,\dots,T_3$, and depth $2$ — the two
towers of §3.

Requiring a depth of at least two leaves $12, 24, 48$; $N = 6$ closes as well but
has depth $1$, a single step from every level to a fixed one, and no room for a
tower. So $12$ is the smallest period that terminates, has a non-trivial fixed
level, and has two generations above its roots. For $N = 12$ that fixed level is
the offset $4$, which is the family $T_2$ YIN MUT of Theorem 3 and the second
root of §2.

Ten, fourteen and eighteen have cycles, and the descent never reaches an end in
them. The period is therefore constrained, not chosen freely; what remains a
choice is which member of the family $12, 24, 48$ one works in.

# 7. What this is, and what it is not

This is a working basis. We claim no depth for the construction, and we state
what it does not settle.

The vocabulary is generated, and the origin is not a choice: it is the set of
levels fixed by the doubling, which cannot be reached from anywhere and must be
posited, and §6 says which levels those are. What §2.1 adds is that the first
generation of one of the two roots reproduces, exactly, a vocabulary fixed
beforehand and independently. What is *not* settled here is why the doubling, of
all maps on offsets, should be the operation; we take it from the system and
derive its consequences.

The counts of §2 are counts of drawn lines in one vocabulary. Changing the origin
changes them. They carry no meaning outside the system that defines them, and we
do not present them as if they did.

Nothing here is a physical statement. The figures are unions of straight lines on
a square; we give no continuous or asymptotic result about them, and nothing in
this paper bears on any object outside the finite construction of §1–§5.

# 8. Related work

Grünbaum and Shephard classified periodic binary fabrics — satins, twills and
their relatives — by their symmetry groups, and catalogued the isonemal ones
[1,2,3]. The parity rule of §5 is an over-and-under interlacement rule: a region
is dark when the number of axes crossed is odd, which is exactly the alternation
that makes a fabric hang together. Their classification proceeds from the
symmetry group of a given fabric; the construction here proceeds from an origin
and one operation, and produces the vocabulary before any figure exists. The two
are complementary, and placing the figures of §5 inside their catalogue — which
are isonemal, which hang together as real cloth — is an obvious next question that
we do not settle.

# 9. Reproducibility

Every number above is printed by a script that reads the published vector
sources and reruns in minutes. Offsets are extracted by exact arithmetic, not by
fitting. The extractor asserts that the $1152$ regions of a plate are occupied
exactly once before any measurement is taken, and refuses files that fail it.
The sweeps of Theorems 4 and 5 are over the complete space, not a sample.
`niveaux_v2.py` prints Proposition 3, Theorem 9 and the count $127$ from
`axes-v2.json` and the catalogue, `codes_cles.py` the ranks $5+8$ and $5+7$, and
`fig_v2.py` draws Figure 3.
